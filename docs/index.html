A research demo for comparing localization methods

This prototype compares two baseline models on synthetic intracranial EEG, displays signal windows, and produces a structured report with a rule-based assistant. The interactive mockup illustrates a proposed clinical workflow; production infrastructure is not implemented.

Decision support, not diagnosis. Every output is evidence with its basis visible. No recommendation exists anywhere in the product. The clinician decides.

The problem

Specialist epilepsy evaluation brings together clinical history, imaging, and recordings. Intracranial EEG samples selected brain locations; interpreting its signals and reconciling model results requires expert review. Onset explores whether visible evidence windows, model comparisons, and structured findings can make that review easier. It has not yet demonstrated time savings or clinical benefit. Read the clinical context, sources, and proposed evaluation.

Planned full-system design

Patient view

Two models side by side per contact; disagreement is flagged as a finding; each rank opens the ten-second evidence window the model scored.

Onset Report

Structured, versioned, immutable. Findings cite predictions and windows; disagreements are stated, not resolved; data quality and limitations are sections; a recommendation is not.

Onset Assistant

Four read-only tools, scoped to the patient on screen. Cites, or refuses. "Which contacts should we resect?" gets the evidence and a refusal.

Runs and registry

Models load by name and version; predictions are immutable rows under a run; mismatched feature versions are refused before scoring.

Trust layers, all in code

Database (immutable rows, row-level security) → contracts → schema validation → output filter and judge → the clinician.

On-prem by design

Open-weight models on a workstation inside the clinic. No patient data leaves the building. De-identification on entry; every access audited.

What the prototype shows today

The app runs on a synthetic five-patient cohort generated with MNE-Python: 48 SEEG contacts each, labeled onset zones, interictal spikes, high-frequency bursts, one seizure, two bad contacts. Two baseline rankers are evaluated leave-one-patient-out; reports and answers use the computed in-memory evidence. The full system is a proposed design.

Research programme

Brain–Machine Interfaces Lab, School of Computing and Artificial Intelligence, Nazarbayev University (PI: Berdakh Abibullaev). Two MSc theses build against Onset's contracts: a localization model that returns scores with evidence windows, and an LLM-driven orchestrator that composes pipeline steps without bypassing a contract. Principles the students inherit: no patient on both sides of a split; every score carries the window it looked at; disagreement is reported, not averaged; nothing recommends treatment; public data first, partner data only through de-identification.

Data: OpenNeuro iEEG (public, BIDS) for development; 15+ patients from a partner medical center to follow, de-identified.
